# Dark Fleet Cyber Risks

## Summary

The dark fleet or shadow fleet is not one single cyber incident. It is a risk pattern involving vessels that may use opaque ownership, poor maintenance, weak insurance, flag changes, AIS manipulation, and sanctions-evasion practices. Cybersecurity matters because these vessels can combine weak digital controls with high physical, environmental, and geopolitical risk.

## Why it belongs in this repo

Dark-fleet activity connects maritime cyber to:

- AIS identity manipulation
- GNSS interference and spoofing exposure
- poor patching and weak onboard controls
- sanctions evasion
- remote-access risk
- undocumented vendor systems
- poor incident reporting
- environmental and safety risk

## Public reporting context

NORMA Cyber and broader maritime-security reporting have highlighted the growing risk around shadow fleets, espionage, cyberattacks, GPS jamming, and disruptive activity around Nordic and European waters.

## Security hypotheses to track

Use this as a watchlist, not as an accusation against any specific vessel without evidence.

Potential risk indicators:

- repeated AIS gaps
- suspicious MMSI or identity changes
- ownership and flag churn
- outdated satellite terminals
- unpatched edge devices
- remote-access tools without proper governance
- inconsistent voyage behavior
- STS transfer patterns linked to sanctions evasion
- weak or missing incident-reporting trails

## Research opportunities

- Build AIS anomaly notebooks for dark-fleet behavior.
- Compare AIS gaps with sanctions and ownership data.
- Track GNSS interference zones and vessel navigation anomalies.
- Study how poor cyber hygiene amplifies environmental and safety risk.
- Separate evidence-based analysis from political claims.

## Caveat

Individual live vessels are intentionally not named. Vessel-level analysis must rely on public data, avoid interference, and respect legal boundaries.
