# TMSA 3 Element 13: Maritime Security and Cyber Risk

Tanker Management and Self Assessment, version 3, is relevant because it connects cyber risk to tanker-operator assurance and commercial vetting expectations. It is not just a technical control checklist; for tanker operators, it can affect customer confidence and chartering relationships.

## Operational relevance

DNV describes cyber risk assessment and mitigating procedures as integral to TMSA3. TMSA may be voluntary in principle, but for tanker operators seeking regular charters from oil majors, meeting its expectations is commercially important.

## Element 13

TMSA 3 added Element 13 for Maritime Security. Industry summaries describe Element 13 as covering physical security, piracy-related concerns, and cybersecurity.

Cybersecurity relevance includes:

- documented cyber-risk management
- integration with security plans
- shore and vessel procedures
- threat identification
- incident-response readiness
- crew awareness
- vendor and remote-access controls

## Use in research and reporting

Use this file when explaining why a finding matters for tanker operators.

Examples:

- Weak remote access to ship-management infrastructure can affect maritime security assurance.
- Missing cyber incident-response procedures can be framed as operational and assurance weakness.
- Poor segmentation between business IT and vessel OT can be mapped to risk-management maturity.

## Caveat

Do not claim a TMSA failure from outside evidence alone. Use TMSA as impact context, not as a legal accusation.
