# NIS2 and Maritime Transport

NIS2 is relevant to maritime because EU transport entities, including maritime and port stakeholders, can fall under cybersecurity risk-management and incident-reporting obligations depending on national implementation.

## What NIS2 adds

ENISA describes NIS2 as strengthening cybersecurity in the transport sector to protect critical infrastructure across aviation, maritime, rail, and road transport.

Source:

- [enisa.europa.eu](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/cybersecurity-of-critical-sectors/transport)

## Why maritime researchers should care

NIS2 matters because maritime organizations are often part of complex multi-entity supply chains:

- ports
- shipping operators
- maritime logistics providers
- terminal operators
- digital service providers
- port-community systems
- satellite and communications providers
- managed service providers

A vulnerability in a maritime system may have NIS2 relevance if it affects availability, continuity, incident reporting, supplier risk, access control, or risk-management governance.

## Practical mapping for this repo

| Security issue | Maritime impact | NIS2-style relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak identity management | Unauthorized access to port or fleet systems | Access control and risk management |
| Poor supplier controls | Third-party compromise of logistics or comms provider | Supply-chain security |
| Missing incident response | Delayed reporting and containment | Incident handling and reporting |
| Unpatched edge devices | Fleet/port remote-access exposure | Technical and organizational measures |
| Poor network segmentation | IT-to-OT movement | Continuity and resilience |

## Caveat

NIS2 is an EU directive and is implemented through member-state law. Avoid saying a specific maritime entity is legally non-compliant unless the member state, sector classification, entity type, and applicable deadlines are verified.
