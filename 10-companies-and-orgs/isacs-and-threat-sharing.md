# ISACs and Maritime Threat-Sharing Organizations

Threat sharing is central to maritime cyber resilience because maritime operations depend on many organizations: shipowners, ports, terminals, flag states, class societies, satcom providers, vendors, and logistics platforms.

## MTS-ISAC

The Maritime Transportation System Information Sharing and Analysis Center exists to strengthen cyber resilience across the maritime ecosystem through trusted information sharing, threat-intelligence collaboration, and coordinated response support.

Sources:

- [mtsisac.org](https://www.mtsisac.org/)
- [mtsisac.org](https://www.mtsisac.org/about)

Operational relevance:

- Maritime-specific cyber threat sharing
- IT, OT, and IIoT focus
- Incident-response coordination support
- Workforce development and training alignment
- Maritime cybersecurity standards support

## NORMA Cyber

NORMA Cyber is the Nordic Maritime Cyber Resilience Centre. It is relevant for Nordic maritime cyber monitoring, threat sharing, and regional maritime resilience.

Sources:

- [normacyber.no](https://www.normacyber.no/)
- [normacyber.no](https://www.normacyber.no/news/the-mts-isac-and-norma-cyber-strengthen-information-sharing-ties)


## Maritime Cyber Attack Database

MCAD is a public maritime cyber-attack database developed by NHL Stenden University of Applied Sciences. It includes incidents dating back to 2001 and reports more than 160 maritime-sector cyber incidents.

Sources:

- [nhlstenden.com](https://www.nhlstenden.com/en/maritime-cyber-attack-database)
- [maritimecybersecurity.nl](https://maritimecybersecurity.nl/info)

Research use:

- Cross-check incident history.
- Avoid relying only on vendor reports.
- Build incident timelines and confidence labels.
