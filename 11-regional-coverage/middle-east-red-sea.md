# Middle East and Red Sea

This region covers the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, Strait of Hormuz, Arabian Gulf, Gulf of Oman, Eastern Mediterranean spillover, and major port hubs in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman, Qatar, Bahrain, Iran, Israel, Egypt, and Yemen-linked maritime corridors.

## Regional context

The Middle East and Red Sea combine physical maritime conflict, energy chokepoints, sanctions enforcement, electronic warfare, dark-fleet activity, and digitalized ports. Cyber risk here is not isolated from geopolitics: GNSS interference, AIS anomalies, sanctions evasion, vessel misidentification, and provider-side communications attacks all affect operational risk.

## Red Sea and Suez routing risk

Since the Red Sea security crisis, carriers have adjusted routing between Suez and the Cape of Good Hope. Reuters reported in July 2026 that Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd began partially returning to Suez Canal sailings under the Gemini network while continuing to monitor security conditions.

## GNSS jamming and spoofing

The region has become one of the most important public examples of maritime GNSS interference.

Relevant public sources:

- Inside GNSS reported that MSC Antonia ran aground near Jeddah in May 2025 and that emerging analysis suggested possible deliberate GNSS interference.
- Marlink reported that Red Sea GPS spoofing rose to dangerous levels and that ship crews were reaching out for support.
- RICS discussed GNSS spoofing and mitigation in the Arabian Gulf, including reports since mid-2025 of GPS jamming and AIS spoofing that placed vessels in sanctioned jurisdictions.
- Lloyd's List reported a surge in GNSS interference across the Middle East Gulf, with vessel impacts and hotspots around Iran, UAE, and Oman.
- Le Monde reported in May 2026 that nearly one million satellite-navigation interference incidents had been recorded since February 28, 2026, affecting around 1,100 ships on the first day of the crisis. Treat this as media-reported, high-level threat context.

## Ports and infrastructure

Major regional port operators and facilities to track include:

- Jebel Ali and DP World-linked ecosystems;
- Khalifa Port and Abu Dhabi logistics infrastructure;
- Saudi Red Sea ports, NEOM/Oxagon, and Ras Al-Khair maritime industry projects;
- Oman ports near Arabian Sea and Strait of Hormuz routes;
- Egypt/Suez Canal Authority and Port Said logistics;
- Israeli ports and Haifa-area GNSS interference research;
- Iranian ports and sanctioned fleet infrastructure.

The IMO Red Sea Project is relevant for broader maritime-security capacity building, even though it is not purely cyber. It aims to develop maritime, port, and law-enforcement security capacity and maritime domain awareness.

Source:

- [imo.org](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/security/pages/redseaproject.aspx)

## Key research themes

- GNSS interference, fallback navigation, bridge-team procedures, and alerting.
- AIS spoofing, AIS identity manipulation, and sanctions-evasion risk.
- VSAT provider compromise and fleet-level communications dependency.
- Port cyber resilience under conflict-adjacent pressure.
- Insurance, rerouting, and operational risk caused by cyber-physical uncertainty.
- Dark-fleet behavior around Iran, Russia-linked oil routes, and sanctions enforcement.
