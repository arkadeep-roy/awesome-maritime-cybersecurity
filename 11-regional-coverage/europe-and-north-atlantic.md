# Europe and North Atlantic

Europe has strong source coverage through ENISA, NIS2, BIMCO/ICS guidance, EMSA, DNV, NORMA Cyber, and several public incidents.

## Regulatory and standards coverage

### ENISA and port cybersecurity

ENISA's 2019 port cybersecurity good-practices report remains one of the strongest public references for port cyber-risk management. It lists threats to the port ecosystem and describes attack scenarios relevant to port authorities and terminal operators.

Sources:

- [enisa.europa.eu](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/port-cybersecurity-good-practices-for-cybersecurity-in-the-maritime-sector)
- [portalcip.org](https://portalcip.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ENISA-Guidelines-Cyber-Risk-Management-for-Ports.pdf)

### NIS2 and transport

ENISA's transport-sector material states that NIS2 highlights the importance of cybersecurity in transport, including maritime, to protect critical infrastructure across transport modes. Do not declare a specific entity in scope without member-state implementation details; NIS2 remains central to maritime cyber governance coverage.

Source:

- [enisa.europa.eu](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/cybersecurity-of-critical-sectors/transport)

### EMSA and ship inspection guidance

EMSA material provides guidance on cybersecurity elements that may be assessed during maritime-security inspections on EU member-state flagged ships. This is relevant for mapping shipboard cyber risk into inspection and compliance workflows.

Source:

- [emsa.europa.eu](https://www.emsa.europa.eu/publications/inventories/download/7660/5074/23.html)

### Italy MIT Circular 177/2025

Italy's MIT Circular No. 177/2025 is an important country-level update. Reporting says it updates cyber-risk measures for national ships, ISM companies, and port facility operators and becomes fully effective from 1 November 2026. Include this as a country-level European regulatory item, but verify against official Italian sources before treating secondary reporting as the legal text.

Sources:

- [informare.it](https://www.informare.it/news/gennews/2026/20260012-MIT-aggiorna-misure-cybersecurity-x-navi-portiuk.asp)
- [adriaports.com](https://www.adriaports.com/en/italy/cyber-risk-at-sea-new-mit-rules-for-ships-and-ports/)
- [gyala.com](https://gyala.com/en/2026/03/11/what-changes-for-ships-and-ports-with-circular-no-177-2025-177-2025/)

## Incidents

### Port of Lisbon, 2022

The Port of Lisbon suffered a cyberattack around Christmas 2022. Public reporting described LockBit claims of stolen financial reports, audits, budgets, contracts, cargo information, ship logs, and port documentation. Some sources said operations were not impacted; keep the impact language specific and source-based.

Sources:

- [porttechnology.org](https://www.porttechnology.org/cyber-attack-threatens-release-of-port-of-lisbon-data/)
- [therecord.media](https://therecord.media/port-of-lisbon-website-still-down-as-lockbit-gang-claims-cyberattack)
- [maritime-executive.com](https://maritime-executive.com/article/cyberattack-threatens-release-of-port-of-lisbon-data)
- [maritimecybersecurity.nl](https://maritimecybersecurity.nl/incident/24b6ye06rL)

## Strategic and geopolitical context

CCDCOE published a policy brief on state-linked cyber threats to critical maritime port infrastructure, emphasizing that ports are critical nodes for global trade and NATO defence logistics. This is relevant for European threat-context framing.
