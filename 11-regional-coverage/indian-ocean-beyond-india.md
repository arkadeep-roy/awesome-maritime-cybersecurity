# Indian Ocean Region beyond India

Coverage includes Indian Ocean Region maritime cybersecurity outside India. India-specific material is tracked separately in `01-standards-and-regulations/india-maritime-cybersecurity.md` and `10-companies-and-orgs/india.md`.

## Regional context

The Indian Ocean Region connects the Strait of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, Mozambique Channel, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Bangladesh, Pakistan, East Africa, and island states such as Mauritius and Seychelles. It is central to energy transit, container shipping, fishing, undersea cables, offshore energy, and naval competition.

An ICWA analysis published in January 2026 cites the Maritime Cyber Attack Database and states that more than 50 cyber-related attacks have been reported in the Indian Ocean. It also notes that the Indian Ocean hosts more than 200 ports, while a large proportion of global oil and container traffic passes through the region.

## Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is strategically relevant because of Colombo Port, Hambantota, Indian Ocean transshipment, and Red Sea rerouting effects. Publicly available maritime-cyber sources remain limited, but there are signs of sector-specific capacity building.

Relevant public items:

- The Colombo Plan Maritime Advisory Programme organized a `Navigating Cyber Threats` workshop in Colombo focused on cybersecurity topics tailored for the maritime sector.
- Sri Lanka Ports Authority-linked media has reported on AI-powered cyber threats in the maritime sector and the need for digital heritage and cyber resilience.

## Bangladesh

Bangladesh is relevant because Chittagong/Chattogram is a major port node and Bangladesh is included in wider Ocean Centres and regional maritime-safety discussions. Public, source-checked cyber details are thin, so avoid overclaiming.

Public maritime-cyber sources for Bangladesh is limited. Further source development should focus on port digitalization, customs and logistics data exchange, national cyber rules affecting ports, and regional maritime-safety capacity-building.

## Pakistan

Pakistan's maritime cyber exposure is relevant through Karachi, Port Qasim, Gwadar, CPEC logistics, and Arabian Sea energy routes. Public maritime-cyber sources are limited in this pass.

Public maritime-cyber sources for Pakistan is limited. Further source development should focus on port digitalization, CPEC/Gwadar infrastructure risk, national CERT material, critical-infrastructure rules, and Arabian Sea AIS/GNSS reliability.

## East Africa, Mauritius, Seychelles, and Maldives

East Africa and island states are relevant because of port modernization, illegal fishing enforcement, cable landings, piracy response, and ocean-governance systems.

Public maritime-cyber sources for East Africa and island states remain uneven. Further source development should focus on Mombasa, Tanzania, Mozambique, Mauritius, Seychelles, Maldives, maritime-domain-awareness systems, port digitization, cyber capacity-building, piracy response, illegal fishing, AIS manipulation, and port IT resilience.

## Region-specific risk themes

- AIS and GNSS trust in congested or contested sea lanes.
- Provider-side risk in satellite communications and fleet connectivity.
- Port community systems and customs data integrity.
- Vessel traffic management and maritime domain awareness.
- Cyber capacity gaps between major hubs and smaller island or coastal states.
- Spillover from Red Sea/Gulf incidents into Indian Ocean routing and insurance decisions.
