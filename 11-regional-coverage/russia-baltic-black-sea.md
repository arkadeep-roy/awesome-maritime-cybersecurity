# Russia, Baltic, and Black Sea

Coverage includes Russia-linked maritime cyber and electronic-warfare issues, the Baltic Sea, Black Sea, Murmansk/Arctic approaches, shadow-fleet risk, GNSS interference, and sanctions-evasion infrastructure.

## Regional context

The Baltic and Black Sea regions combine military tension, energy infrastructure, sanctions enforcement, shadow-fleet activity, subsea cables, port logistics, and repeated GNSS interference. Cyber and electronic warfare cannot be cleanly separated here: AIS manipulation, GNSS spoofing, port disruption, drone surveillance, and vessel identity fraud all influence maritime risk.

## GNSS and AIS anomaly evidence

SeaSpoofFinder's 2026 AIS-based GNSS spoofing detection paper reports recurrent potential spoofing patterns in several regions, including the Baltic Sea, Black Sea, Murmansk, Moscow, and Haifa. The paper emphasizes that AIS-only evidence is relevant for identifying candidate events but does not prove attribution.

Sources:

- [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16257)
- [seaspooffinder.github.io](https://seaspooffinder.github.io/ais_data)

## Shadow fleet and registry abuse

European enforcement against Russia-linked shadow-fleet vessels is creating new maritime cyber and data-integrity questions. Reuters reported in July 2026 that Europe targeted tankers falsely using the Cameroon flag and that Cameroon deregistered 39 such vessels after fraudulent registry misuse. This is relevant to cybersecurity because registry integrity, AIS identity, sanctions data, and maritime-domain-awareness systems are all information-security problems.
