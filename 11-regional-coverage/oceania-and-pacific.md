# Oceania and Pacific Islands

Coverage includes Australia, New Zealand, and Pacific Island maritime cybersecurity: port logistics, offshore energy, AUKUS-adjacent infrastructure, Pacific cyber capacity-building, illegal fishing enforcement, undersea cables, and small-island dependency on maritime trade.

## Australia

### DP World Australia cyber incident, 2023

DP World Australia stated that on 10 November 2023 its technology team detected unauthorized access to the Australian corporate network and disconnected the network from the internet to contain the incident. DP World said the incident impacted land-side port operations while containment was underway.

Sources:

- [dpworld.com](https://www.dpworld.com/en/news/releases/australia/media-statement-update-on-cybersecurity-incident)
- [abc.net.au](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-11-11/dp-world-australian-ports-cyber-security-incident/103094358)
- [cybercx.com.au](https://cybercx.com.au/news/freight-giant-dp-world-recovers-from-cyber-attack/)

### Critical ports and SOCI obligations

Australia's Security of Critical Infrastructure framework applies to critical infrastructure sectors, including critical ports. The Cyber and Infrastructure Security Centre factsheet for critical ports describes cyber incidents such as unauthorized access, unauthorized modification, and impairment of computer availability, reliability, security, or operation. It also describes cyber-incident reporting obligations for responsible entities.

Source:

- [cisc.gov.au](https://www.cisc.gov.au/resources-subsite/Documents/cisc-factsheet-soci-act-obligations-for-critical-ports.pdf)

### Australia-Pacific security context

Australia's Pacific security cooperation includes cyber security as part of a broader whole-of-government approach covering defence, policing, law enforcement, border management, disaster resilience, and human security. This matters because Pacific port resilience is often tied to regional capacity-building rather than local maritime cyber vendors.

Source:

- [dfat.gov.au](https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/pacific/shared-security-in-the-pacific)

## New Zealand

Public maritime-specific cyber material is thinner than Australia; New Zealand coverage is organized around:

- port operators and logistics systems;
- critical infrastructure reform;
- maritime border and customs platforms;
- Pacific cyber capacity-building;
- Australia-New Zealand security cooperation.

New Zealand's 2026 critical infrastructure cyber discussion document is relevant as broader context, though not maritime-specific in this pass.

Source:

- [dpmc.govt.nz](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-03/nz-cyber-security-discussion-doc-feb-2026-v2.pdf)

## Pacific Islands

Pacific Island maritime cyber risk is tied to:

- port digitization and customs systems;
- fisheries monitoring and illegal fishing enforcement;
- undersea cables and satellite connectivity;
- cyber capacity-building;
- disaster response and supply-chain continuity;
- geopolitical competition around security partnerships.

Australia's shared-security material explicitly includes cyber security in Pacific cooperation. New Zealand has also supported cyber capacity building in the Pacific.

Sources:

- [dfat.gov.au](https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/pacific/shared-security-in-the-pacific)
- [cybilportal.org](https://cybilportal.org/projects/new-zealand-cyber-security-capacity-building-in-the-pacific-programme/)

## AUKUS and port automation context

Public reporting in 2026 highlighted concerns around Western Australia's Westport project, port automation, proximity to HMAS Stirling, and AUKUS-related security considerations. Treat this as strategic context, not a confirmed cyber incident.

Source:

- [theaustralian.com.au](https://www.theaustralian.com.au/nation/maritime-unions-fresh-warning-on-aukus-security-risk-labour-supply/news-story/6bbc20dcdb13c35e05cc449fbd8369cf)

## Research themes

- Critical port obligations and incident reporting.
- Ransomware and corporate-network compromise affecting port land-side operations.
- Port automation and national-security dependencies.
- Pacific cyber capacity-building for ports, customs, fisheries, and undersea-cable resilience.
